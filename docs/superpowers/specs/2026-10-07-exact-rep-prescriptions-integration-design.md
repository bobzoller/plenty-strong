# Plenty Strong exact-rep prescriptions: integration design

Status: proposed for Bob's review together with the implementation plan. This is planning, not an implemented or deployed policy.

Authority: [Trainer consultation](2026-10-07-exact-rep-prescriptions-trainer-consult.md), supplied by Bob October 7, 2026. Preserve that document verbatim. It supersedes the rep-ceiling-only policy for new-policy sessions; it does not change the interpretation of recorded sessions. The existing fixed exercise selection and opaque movement-variant design remain in force.

## Intent and scope

People want prescriptions. Each movement shows previous actual performance, today's exact reps per set and prescribed load, and blank inputs for actual performance. Targets are attempts, not promises of improvement. Effort, pain, and control always override the number. Progression is deterministic, offline, and independent of an LLM, account, subscription, telemetry, tips, or cloud availability.

Implement the consultation's recommended conditional double progression, not its alternatives. Do not add accessories, numeric effective bodyweight resistance, programmable progression settings, a readiness questionnaire, or changes to Bob's personal training records. No cloud provisioning, signing, phone replacement, push, merge, or deployment is part of implementation approval.

## Required policy and precedence

Use the consultation's sections 2–8 and all 19 worked examples as normative new-policy requirements. The nominal floor is a fitting threshold; later sets may have goals below it, minimum one. Normal goal ranges, doses, rest, and the fixed Sunday/Tuesday/Thursday schedule retain their existing presets. Low-rep strength starts at 4, other/fallback work at 8; ordinary ceilings start at 12, low-rep strength at 6, and extension limits are 20 and 8 respectively.

Evaluate: pain/control or retained shared pause; return/easier execution rules; changed or mixed load and incomplete/unequal/unknown observations; baseline fitting; strain; above-goal adoption/shortfalls; ceiling confirmations/load or ceiling changes; ordinary rep progression. Safety and too-hard handling override overshoot handling. A skipped exercise/easier session can freeze counters; partial/unknown normal work clears both confirmations and consecutive shortfall/strain sequences. Changing exact numerical goals alone does not change comparison context.

Normal copy is exactly: “Aim for the listed reps. Stop at that number, or sooner when another good rep would leave fewer than about two reps in reserve. Stop earlier for pain or loss of control.” For a stricter reserve store and display: “Aim for the listed reps. Stop at that number, or sooner to keep at least {minimumRir} good reps left. Stop earlier for pain or loss of control.” Easier/return reserve is at least 4, increased by any stricter movement restriction.

Ask “How hard were the working sets?” Too easy means every set left substantially more reserve than intended; About right means appropriately challenging with no set too hard; Too hard means at least one set could not preserve the reserve; I'm not sure is unknown. Preserve the legacy `finalEffort` wire field, with explicit new `effortScope = all_working_sets`; never retrofit that scope to old observations.

Store prior goals P independently of raw actuals A. A complete effort-limited miss repeats P once, rebases to A on the second consecutive comparable miss, and receives no increment. Time/interruption or unknown reasons do not establish this streak. All-goals-met on-target building work adds one TOTAL rep to the lowest eligible set, ties earliest; too-easy work adds two per set within the ceiling. Maintenance on-target holds. First too-hard work sets `max(1, A[i]-1)`, capped at the ceiling; second comparable strain reduces to the nearest lower available load and starts baseline at the preset floor.

## Explicit interpretations requiring plan review

These close underspecified implementation cases. They are proposed engineering/product decisions, not additional findings from the Trainer or research.

1. **Overshoots:** some goals exceeded with all goals met, non-hard known work within the ceiling adopts A, resets ceiling confirmation, and receives no bonus. Unknown/conflicting work holds P. Above-ceiling work holds P and flags review except when safety/too-hard handling takes precedence. Too-easy feedback alongside a missed goal holds P and flags an observation conflict.
2. **Floor misses:** only the first working set below the nominal floor counts as a load-suitability strain, and only in complete, controlled, known-effort work. First non-hard floor miss holds P (baseline remains baseline); a second consecutive comparable strain, whether hard-effort or first-set-floor, lowers load. Later-set floor misses alone are neither strain nor grounds to reject a baseline such as 8/7/6. Below-floor plus too-easy is conflicting feedback and holds rather than advancing.
3. **No lower load/bodyweight setback:** after the second strain with no lower numeric load, mark this variant as needing setup review and emit no working-set prescription. Preserve load, goals, history and shared safety. A newly created variant begins its own baseline; an explicit setup reset may restart the reviewed variant. Do not repeatedly reduce reps automatically. This setup limit is distinct from a pain/control safety pause.
4. **Changed actual load:** accept a supported confirmed actual load as raw performance, clear comparisons, and prescribe the preset floor in baseline at that load next time. Do not fit capacity against targets issued for a different load. Mixed loads are explicitly flagged noncomparable; the existing stop-for-load-correction path preserves observations rather than restarting the exercise invisibly.
5. **Per-set gaps:** preserve an explicit set index on performed observations and a separate list of skipped set indices. Skips are absent performances, not zero-rep sets. A skipped internal set makes that exercise partial. Per-side asymmetry remains raw left/right observations and cannot qualify upward progression.
6. **Interruption clock:** use the last suitable normal completion or successful normal baseline, known/non-hard/controlled at a confirmed load (or bodyweight), not easier/partial/unknown sessions. No evidence date means baseline, not a fabricated 28-day absence. At the 28-day boundary retain normal goals independently, return with one fewer set (minimum one), goal prefix capped by preset floor, and highest available load at or below 90% of previous load. If no such lower equipment value exists, retain the minimum available current load. Clean non-hard return restores normal sets in baseline at the returned load, targeting preset floor; it contributes no ceiling confirmation.
7. **Set changes:** a count-only change within the same goal/range retains the prefix, appending successively `max(1, lastGoal-1)` when growing. Goal/range changes instead seed the new preset floor and clear comparisons. Do not add a user set-count editor or change the fixed schedule. Implement and test the resizing primitive for supported engine configuration transitions and synthetic count-only exercise-state fixtures.
8. **Activation:** legacy final-set effort and ceiling prescriptions cannot qualify exact-policy comparisons. Preserve records, loads, setup identity, restrictions, pauses, event IDs and dates; seed each variant at its preset floor in baseline, with empty comparison windows. Use the latest legacy normal completion/calibration date only to conservatively decide whether a return is already due, never as evidence of all-set effort or exact goals. Never discard or rewrite an existing draft. Finish/discard an eligible empty draft through existing explicit controls, then activate at the next-workout boundary.

## Versioning and data boundaries

- Keep `general-fitness-swift1`, `general-fitness-v0.2`, their bundled JSON, hashes, exact copy, fixtures and replay behavior unchanged.
- Introduce immutable `general-fitness-exact-v1`, contract version 3, ProgramState/JournalEnvelope schema 3, and `RulesetCatalog.exactV1()`. Continue using the fixed profile and its hash. Pin the new manifest hash after computing canonical JSON without its hash field; it is not an arbitrary constant.
- Add an exhaustive supported schema/policy capability switch: schema 1 numeric, schema 2 fixed ceilings, schema 3 fixed exact goals. Unknown pairs reject; no `>= 2` fallback. Version dispatch must cover variants, shared safety, schedule, configuration, baseline/return, journal and backup/recovery admission.
- New optional wire fields default nil and are omitted when absent, preserving canonical legacy bytes. Schema 3 validation requires their relevant content. No new SwiftData tables are needed: existing immutable payloads are opaque Data.
- Activate through one typed append-only journal command linking the unchanged schema-2 parent to the schema-3 result, with source/destination rules archives and atomic head/outbox persistence. Active drafts/frozen Finish payloads, stale revision/head, or non-ready store health block activation. New programs start exact policy directly.
- Replay each envelope using its original pinned rules; policy activation alone bridges the version boundary. Export/import preserve legacy prefixes and new records exactly. Both portable backup formats and local fake-cloud recovery must support the new policy and fail closed on unknown future versions.
- **Deliberate deferred capability:** divergent heads spanning a policy boundary (including a schema-2 common ancestor with independent activations) retain every original, block working admission, and report `mixed_policy_conflict`. Do not choose/downgrade a head or merge comparison windows automatically. Ordinary same-policy conflicts still use existing explicit resolution with unioned safety. Full cross-policy conflict resolution is separate work before live cloud rollout; this plan does not activate cloud.
