**To: Bob / Tech Lead**  
**Subject: Recommended second starter program**

I recommend offering **“Upper-body emphasis”** and **“Whole-body, glute emphasis.”** Both should be available to anyone. The distinction is where we allocate training time and effort—not a men’s program versus a women’s program.

The second option should distribute lower-body work across three days, retain regular upper-body training, and include a simple glute-focused movement. Below is one concrete recommendation. Its principles are evidence-informed; the exact exercise combination, set counts, and numerical defaults are coaching and product decisions, not a research-validated protocol.

**1. Program choice and descriptions**

| Choice | Suggested description |
|---|---|
| **Upper-body emphasis** | “Whole-body training with extra attention to your chest, back, shoulders, and arms.” |
| **Whole-body, glute emphasis** | “Whole-body training with extra attention to your glutes, while building your legs and upper body.” |

“Balanced, with glute emphasis” is also reasonable, provided “balanced” means broad coverage rather than equal sets for every muscle.

Keep **physique emphasis** separate from **training goal**. Someone can choose glute emphasis while pursuing strength, muscle growth, muscle preservation during weight loss, or maintenance.

A 2025 meta-analysis of 29 studies found broadly similar **relative muscle growth** in men and women, although absolute gains favored men overall. That supports common training principles; it does not establish an optimal sex-specific routine. Most participants were young or middle-aged, and evidence in experienced trainees was limited. **My recommendation:** use preferences and actual capability to select the program and starting loads. [Refalo et al., 2025](https://peerj.com/articles/19042/)

**2. Recommended weekly schedule**

Use **Sunday / Tuesday / Thursday**, with six exercises per session, performed in this order.

The table gives the established **muscle-growth preset**. Beginners start with two working sets for every movement before the introductory transition described below.

| Order | Sunday | Tuesday | Thursday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DB Romanian deadlift — **3 sets** | Supported static split squat — **2 sets per leg** | Suitcase DB squat — **3 sets** |
| 2 | Incline DB press — **3 sets** | DB floor glute bridge — **2 sets** | DB Romanian deadlift — **3 sets** |
| 3 | Chest-supported DB row — **3 sets** | Chest-supported DB row — **3 sets** | Incline DB press — **3 sets** |
| 4 | DB floor glute bridge — **2 sets** | DB lateral raise — **2 sets** | Chest-supported DB row — **3 sets** |
| 5 | Supported single-leg calf raise — **2 sets per leg** | Supported single-leg calf raise — **2 sets per leg** | DB lateral raise — **2 sets** |
| 6 | Dead bug heel tap — **2 sets** | Standing DB curl — **2 sets** | Dead bug heel tap — **2 sets** |

Use a roughly **30° incline** for pressing and chest-supported rowing, retaining a consistent setup. The split squat has **both feet on the floor**, with one hand supporting against a stable bench and one dumbbell in the other hand.

This distributes glute work across all three days. Squats and split squats provide quadriceps work; RDLs provide hamstring work; bridges add glute-focused work without adding another demanding standing compound. Pressing, rowing, shoulders, calves, and direct trunk work remain regular parts of the week.

The glute emphasis comes from **exercise selection, weekly allocation, and placing lower-body work early**. Three weekly exposures are a convenient way to distribute the work, not a claim that frequency itself guarantees more growth.

A nine-week supervised trial in 34 untrained young adults found similar glute growth from barbell squats and hip thrusts, with greater thigh growth from squats. This supports retaining squat work while including hip-extension work. It did **not** test dumbbell floor bridges, this combination, or home training; choosing the floor bridge is a practical coaching judgment. [Plotkin et al., 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

**3. Reps, rest, and effort**

| Movement | Normal rep range | Rest between working sets |
|---|---:|---:|
| RDL, incline press, chest-supported row, suitcase squat, supported split squat | 8–12 | 120 seconds |
| Floor glute bridge | 10–15 | 120 seconds |
| Lateral raise, calf raise | 10–15 | 90 seconds |
| Standing curl | 8–12 | 90 seconds |
| Dead bug heel tap | 6–10 per side | 60 seconds |

These ranges are the engine’s operating bounds. The workout interface still displays **exact per-set goals**.

Keep the existing common instruction:

> “Stop at your goal, or earlier when you think you could do two more good reps. Stop for pain or loss of control.”

For trunk work, “good reps” means repetitions completed without losing the prescribed back position or control. Do not require beginners to discover muscular failure through broken technique.

**Research finding:** failure is not necessary for useful results. A small eight-week study in trained adults found similar quadriceps growth with failure and 1–2 RIR, with greater acute fatigue from failure. **App default:** approximately two good reps left across these movements. The trial does not establish an exact universal optimum for every exercise or beginner. [Refalo et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/)

The established size preset contains **44 working sets weekly**, distributed 15/13/16 across the sessions. The two-set preset contains 36. Budget approximately **45–65 minutes** including preparation and equipment changes; measure actual completion times before promising shorter sessions. No required supersets initially.

**4. Weekly muscle exposure**

A unilateral set below means one set completed on **both sides**. Two sets per leg provide two sets of work for each leg—not four sets per muscle.

| Muscle/group | Two-set starter/preservation preset | Established size preset | Days/week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Glute maximus | 4 bridge sets + 8 compound sets | 4 bridge sets + 11 compound sets | 3 |
| Quadriceps | 4 compound sets | 5 compound sets | 2 |
| Hamstrings | 4 RDL sets | 6 RDL sets | 2 |
| Chest | 4 press sets | 6 press sets | 2 |
| Back | 6 row sets | 9 row sets | 3 |
| Lateral delts | 4 direct sets | 4 direct sets | 2 |
| Calves | 4 direct sets | 4 direct sets | 2 |
| Trunk | 4 direct sets, plus compound bracing | Same | 2 direct |
| Biceps | 2 curl sets, plus rowing | Same direct work; more rowing | 1 direct, 3 indirect |
| Triceps | No isolation; pressing contribution | No isolation; more pressing | 2 indirect |

The glute compound count comprises RDLs, squats, and split squats. These muscles are intended prime movers, but a compound set is not equivalent to a glute-isolation set in every trainee.

Do not add the column entries together to calculate total workout sets: the same squat appears under both glutes and quadriceps.

Research supports a volume–response relationship with diminishing returns and benefits from distinguishing direct and indirect work. Fractional accounting improved aggregate predictions in one recent meta-analysis; it does **not** prove that every indirect set contributes exactly half a direct set. Its sample was predominantly young men. Treat this table as a transparent planning estimate. [Pelland et al., published 2025; 2026 issue](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w)

There are acknowledged gaps: no knee-flexion hamstring exercise, direct hip-abduction exercise, vertical pull, or triceps isolation. This is broad starter coverage with glute-max emphasis, not maximum development of every muscle region.

**5. Goal compatibility**

The schedule and emphasis stay recognizable across goals.

| Goal | Recommended preset |
|---|---|
| **Muscle growth** | Established sets shown above; normal rep ranges. |
| **Preserve muscle while losing weight** | Two sets per movement; normal ranges. Retaining useful performance is success—progress is welcome, not compulsory. |
| **Strength** | Established set counts. Press, row, and suitcase squat use 4–6 reps where safe loading and handling are established; RDL uses 6–10. Rest 180 seconds for these movements. Other movements retain normal ranges. |
| **Maintenance** | Two sets per movement on the same three-day schedule. Hold successful prescriptions according to the maintenance policy; no compulsory escalation. |

These are **app defaults**. Lower-rep strength eligibility should depend on the movement and safe implement handling, not just the selected goal. A four-rep dumbbell squat limited by grip or getting the weights into position is not automatically better programming.

ACSM’s 2026 guidance supports heavier loading for strength, greater weekly volume for hypertrophy, and progressive training without mandatory failure or elaborate periodization. Its broad twice-weekly muscle-group guidance fits this distribution. Neither the position stand nor its summary validates these exact presets. [ACSM position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/), [official practical guidance](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

Resistance exercise during dietary weight loss helps preserve fat-free mass, according to a review of adults with overweight or obesity. That supports the preservation goal, but does not guarantee zero muscle loss or establish this exact two-set dose. [2025 weight-loss review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40909191/)

Maintenance may require less work than building, but evidence shows population-dependent results. Retaining two sets is a conservative product choice, not a universal minimum. [Bickel et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/)

**6. Beginner setup and essential cues**

Do not prescribe Bob’s weights or calculate starting weights from sex or body weight.

Give one guided setup: practice the movement unloaded or lightly loaded, then confirm a load that allows the initial goal with controlled technique and roughly two good reps remaining. The app supplies subsequent decisions.

For every new beginner, start at **two working sets**. For size/strength, move an eligible movement to three sets after **two complete, pain-free, controlled, non-hard comparable exposures** at two sets. Use the existing set-change baseline handling, reset comparison evidence, and do not simultaneously increase load. This introductory transition is a product heuristic; avoid further automatic set escalation.

| Movement | Essential cue or practical limitation |
|---|---|
| RDL | Soft knees; move hips back; keep dumbbells close. Stop the descent before losing repeatable trunk position. Reaching the floor is unnecessary. |
| Suitcase squat | Dumbbells at your sides; sit down between your feet within a comfortable range. Handling, grip, and dumbbell clearance can limit loading. |
| Supported split squat | Both feet remain on the floor. Use light hand support, lower under control, and start with the less capable side. |
| Floor bridge | Keep ribs down; extend the hips without arching the lower back. Secure one dumbbell over the hip crease with comfortable padding. |
| Press and row | Keep bench angle consistent. Control the lowering and use a comfortable range; avoid bouncing or forcing the shoulder position. |
| Calf raise | Use a stable bench for hand support, one dumbbell in the free hand, and controlled ankle movement. No improvised step required. |
| Lateral raise and curl | Comfortable elbow position; avoid turning the repetition into a whole-body swing. |
| Dead bug heel tap | Maintain a comfortable, stable lower-back position while alternating controlled heel taps. Log repetitions per side. |

The bridge needs a **setup check**: some adjustable dumbbells are too bulky or uncomfortable to secure on the pelvis. If that happens, prescribe a bodyweight bridge variant initially. Do not assume every “adjustable dumbbell” is suitable for every exercise.

I would avoid making bench hip thrusts, rear-foot-elevated split squats, hanging leg raises, or unassisted pull-ups mandatory starter movements. They introduce setup, balance, accessory, or strength requirements that the stated equipment does not resolve.

**7. Compatibility with exact-rep progression**

The progression engine can remain conceptually unchanged. The program builder must supply the movement-specific sets, ranges, rest, and low-rep eligibility above.

Important details:

- **Load basis:** paired-dumbbell exercises log pounds per hand. Bridge and supported unilateral exercises log the single dumbbell’s weight. Do not double either silently.
- **Per-side repetitions:** split squats, calf raises, and heel taps need explicit per-side meaning. Preserve unequal actuals; unfinished or unequal work cannot confirm upward progression.
- **Bodyweight:** retain null numeric resistance. Different assistance, leverage, or setup is an independent saved variant.
- **Large increments:** retain the existing confirmation and rep-ceiling fallback. A 20→25 lb jump is 25%; the program does not justify bypassing the engine’s automatic-step limit.
- **Equipment ceiling:** an 80 lb bridge or 160 lb paired RDL may eventually become limiting. A harder variant requires deliberate selection and a new baseline; never silently substitute it.
- **Repeated movements:** retain consistent setup and order. Shared progression can continue across days, but persistent differences caused by session position should separate comparable histories rather than generate repeated false setbacks.

Changing programs should preserve actual history. Preserve a familiar movement’s usable load, while resetting comparisons when its set count, rest, setup, or preceding workload changes materially.

More frequent practice may create more progression opportunities. It does not create an obligation to improve every workout.

This is a **fictional established size-preset week**, using previously calibrated loads. It is not a starting-weight recommendation.

Assume each goal below is completed with controlled technique and “about right” effort; repeated movements therefore illustrate the previously proposed one-total-rep progression.

| Order | Sunday goal | Tuesday goal | Thursday goal |

For the Sunday RDL:

| Stage | Prescription or observation |
|---|---|
| **Last time** | Actual 30 lb/hand, **10/10/8**, complete and “about right” |
| **Today’s goal** | 30 lb/hand, **10/10/9** |
| **Recorded actuals** | **10/10/9**, complete and “about right” |
| **Next prescription** | 30 lb/hand, **10/10/10** |

If Thursday’s actuals instead become **10/10/8** because the user reaches the stopping boundary, preserve those actuals. Under the existing first-miss policy, repeat **10/10/10** next time. The missed goal does not require forced repetitions.

Under the previously proposed policy, Sunday’s same **10/10/9** with “too easy” feedback would produce **12/12/11**, bounded by the current ceiling. These integer transitions remain **product rules**, not research-validated forecasts.

**9. Changes needed before presenting the existing routine as a general starter**

I would make some changes. Its lower-body and direct trunk work are concentrated on one day, and its assistance/accessory requirements are easy to underestimate.

A small redistribution worth reviewing is:

- Replace Sunday’s lying-curl slot with an RDL.
- Replace Thursday’s triceps-extension slot with a suitcase squat.
- Keep Tuesday’s lower-body work, using the supported split squat and a floor-based trunk default.
- Make pull-up/chin-up assistance feasibility explicit; do not assume beginners can perform them with only a bar.

This retains more upper-body emphasis while providing a second quadriceps and hamstring exposure. It is a proposed revision, not an approved replacement. Calf and direct-trunk coverage still need explicit treatment if we advertise twice-weekly coverage of every major group.

Also, do not import Bob’s personal injury restrictions, failure prescription, or deload calendar as universal starter rules. Both app options should use the app’s common effort and recovery policies.

**10. Expectations and future expansion**

Reasonable messaging is:

> “A simple whole-body program that gives your glutes extra attention. Build strength and muscle, or maintain useful muscle and performance while losing weight.”

Do not promise a particular butt shape, regional fat loss, a growth rate, or superiority over every alternative. Most emphasis here concerns glute maximus; it is not a validated program for changing every glute region. Maintenance preserves capability rather than promising reshaping.

For later expansion, keep **goal, physique preference, equipment, schedule, experience, and practical limitations** as distinct inputs. Sex may inform relevant context, but should not silently determine physique preference or starting load.

Store program versions, movement variants, load basis, per-side counting, and progression eligibility explicitly. That will make future equipment options and program changes manageable without redesigning the progression engine.

This recommendation is ready for program/specification review. It does not change Bob’s personal training plan.
